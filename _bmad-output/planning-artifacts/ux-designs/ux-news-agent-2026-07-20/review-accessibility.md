# Accessibility Review - news-agent digest email (Midnight direction)

## Overall verdict

The spine correctly fixes the prior serif-readability complaint and gets the highest-traffic text (body bullets, intro, scan block, headline, gold kickers) to strong WCAG AA contrast (8–19:1) against the `#0b1020` background - this part of the design is genuinely solid. However, the Accessibility Floor in EXPERIENCE.md checks only one color pair (`ink-body` vs background) and that single check gives false confidence: three other color pairs *documented in the same DESIGN.md* actually fail WCAG AA when computed - the cybersecurity tag red, the default fallback tag slate, and the footer text color. There are also unaddressed gaps around decorative-font legibility (not just fallback), bidi mixing of English brand names in RTL Hebrew, and semantic heading structure for screen readers.

## Findings

- **high** Default fallback topic tag color `tag-default` (`#475569`) on background `#0b1020` computes to **~2.5:1** contrast, failing WCAG AA for both normal text (needs 4.5:1) and large text (needs 3:1). (DESIGN.md:21, DESIGN.md:158 - color table; EXPERIENCE.md:45 - states this fallback "never" renders empty/broken, i.e. it's a designed, permanent, recurring state, not a rare edge case). *Fix:* Lighten the fallback slate (e.g. toward `#94a3b8`/`#cbd5e1`, both of which land above 4.5:1 on this background) and re-verify; add this pair to the Accessibility Floor's checked list.

- **high** Cybersecurity tag color `tag-cybersecurity` (`#dc2626`) on `#0b1020` computes to **~3.9:1**, failing the 4.5:1 requirement for small text (tags render at 10px per `typography.article-tag` - nowhere near the 18px/14px-bold "large text" carve-out). This is one of only three fixed, permanent topic colors, per the Do's ("Keep topic tag colors consistent... not only in this email"), so it will render in every cybersecurity-tagged digest indefinitely. (DESIGN.md:19, DESIGN.md:156; DESIGN.md:72-78 article-tag typography spec). *Fix:* Brighten the red (e.g. `#f87171` / `#ef4444` lands closer to or above 4.5:1) - pure saturated red has low luminance-weighted contrast on near-black regardless of how vivid it looks; verify computationally, not by eye.

- **medium** Footer text color (`typography.footer`, `#6b7590`) on `#0b1020` computes to **~4.1:1**, just under the 4.5:1 normal-text threshold (12px, not large text). (DESIGN.md:105-110). *Fix:* Nudge lighter (e.g. `#7c86a3` or reuse `ink-dim` `#a3adc4` which already passes at ~8.4:1) and re-verify.

- **medium** The Accessibility Floor (EXPERIENCE.md:66-71) verifies only `ink-body` vs `background` and generalizes with "Any future palette or type change must re-verify this ratio" - but as the three findings above show, several *other* pairs already fail in the current, approved DESIGN.md, undetected by that single check. The floor gives an implementer false confidence that contrast has been handled system-wide. *Fix:* Either list every text/background color pair that must clear 4.5:1 (or 3:1 for large text) explicitly in the Accessibility Floor - tags, footer, source line, all typography colors - or state a general rule ("every color in the `colors` token table used for text must be verified against every background it appears on") rather than naming a single instance.

- **medium** Gveret Levin's *fallback* (font fails to load) is thoroughly specified as load-bearing (DESIGN.md:166, EXPERIENCE.md:70), but the spine never addresses the parallel risk that the decorative font, when it *does* load, is itself a connected handwriting/cursive face - the same category of readability risk that got Frank Ruhl Libre rejected (`קשה לי לקרוא`, .memlog.md:29). Nothing caps the punchline's length or complexity to mitigate this; the actual mockup punchline is a two-sentence, ~130-character joke (.working/midnight.html:108) - long for a decorative script face rendered at 26px. (DESIGN.md:166, EXPERIENCE.md:70 - both stop at "fallback must not produce tofu," neither says "keep the punchline short because the display face itself has limited legibility"). *Fix:* Add an explicit guideline - e.g. "keep the קינוח punchline to one short sentence (~10-12 words / ~60 chars) to limit legibility risk from the decorative face," mirroring the reasoning that already drove the Rubik switch.

- **medium** No guidance on Unicode bidi isolation for embedded LTR runs (English brand names, source names) inside RTL Hebrew sentences/labels. The mockup itself demonstrates the exact pattern: `OpenAI חושפת מודל...` (Latin brand name leading a Hebrew sentence) and `TechCrunch · 2 דק׳ קריאה · <a>למקור באנגלית ←</a>` (.working/midnight.html:84, 90). Mixed-direction runs with adjacent punctuation/numbers (e.g. "40%" in a bullet, .working/midnight.html:88) are a known bidi-reordering risk in HTML email clients. Neither DESIGN.md nor EXPERIENCE.md mentions `<bdi>`/`dir="ltr"` wrapping or Unicode isolate characters (U+2066/U+2069) for these embedded runs, despite `dir="rtl"` being called out as the sole bidi-relevant requirement (EXPERIENCE.md:68). *Fix:* Add a line to the Accessibility Floor requiring English brand/source names and URLs embedded in Hebrew copy to be wrapped in `<bdi>` (or `<span dir="ltr">`) in the Jinja template.

- **low** No semantic heading structure specified or used. The mockup's masthead title, scan-block heading, and article headline are all plain `<div>`s styled to look like headings (`.title`, `.scan-h`, `.ah` - .working/midnight.html:25,31,38), and the pre-existing production template (`src/newsagent/templates/digest.html.j2`) follows the same div-only pattern. Neither spine doc mandates `<h1>`–`<h3>` (or ARIA heading roles) anywhere, so screen-reader users who navigate HTML emails by heading landmarks get no structure to jump through. (DESIGN.md Components section, EXPERIENCE.md Component Patterns - neither mentions heading semantics). *Fix:* Specify heading tags for masthead title, scan-block label, and article headline in the Component Patterns table.

- **low** No spine requirement for empty `alt=""` on the tracking-pixel image. EXPERIENCE.md:62 mentions the tracking pixel only as a silent request that records `opened_at`; DESIGN.md doesn't mention it at all. The current (pre-Midnight) `digest.html.j2` already correctly ships `alt=""` (line 83), but since the spine never locks this in as a requirement, a future rewrite of the template for Midnight could regress it (a missing/non-empty `alt` on a 1×1 pixel gets announced by some screen readers as a meaningless image). *Fix:* Add one line to EXPERIENCE.md's Interaction Primitives or Component Patterns: "tracking pixel `<img>` must carry `alt=\"\"`."

- **low** Accessibility Floor's contrast requirement is phrased with an approximate qualifier ("must stay above **~4.5:1**") and the line-height fix is described only directionally ("a deliberate contrast/line-height increase") without pinning a firm minimum, weakening enforceability against future regressions even though DESIGN.md's typography table does record concrete line-height values (1.5–1.7) that could be cited as the floor. (EXPERIENCE.md:69). *Fix:* Replace "~4.5:1" with a hard "must meet or exceed 4.5:1 (WCAG AA)" and reference the specific line-height values (e.g. "body/bullet line-height must not drop below 1.5") as the enforceable floor.

## What's solid

- `dir="rtl" lang="he"` is explicitly required in the Accessibility Floor and correctly present on `<html>` in the mockup.
- Topic tags genuinely carry both a color AND a text label - verified true in EXPERIENCE.md's Component Patterns table (not just asserted in DESIGN.md), including the "never an empty or broken tag" fallback behavior.
- The core reading-path colors are comfortably above WCAG AA when computed: `ink-body` #cfd7e8 ~13.1:1, `ink-dim` #a3adc4 ~8.4:1, intro/scan-item text ~14–14.6:1, headline white ~18.9:1, and - contrary to the intuitive worry that a gold accent on a near-black background might be risky - gold `#f0b429` actually computes to ~10.2:1 against the base background and ~6.9:1 against the fortune-card's lighter navy gradient stop, both comfortably passing even at small kicker sizes.
- Gveret Levin's *fallback-on-block* behavior (Outlook desktop / mobile clients that block external fonts) is explicitly called "load-bearing, not cosmetic" with a literal fallback stack in both DESIGN.md and the mockup - this is the one font-risk dimension that is well handled.
- The Frank Ruhl Libre rejection is traced to its actual root cause (readability, with the user's own words `קשה לי לקרוא` preserved) in both DESIGN.md and EXPERIENCE.md, not just recorded as a preference change - good traceability for future editors.
- Source name and reading time are explicitly required to stay in plain text, never color-only (Accessibility Floor).
- LLM-provided bullet/keyword content must be `html.escape`-d before `**markdown**`→`<strong>` conversion, preventing raw HTML/script injection from provider output - a correctness/robustness protection that also guards against screen-reader-breaking malformed markup.
